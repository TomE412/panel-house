Spine-and-Pillar Panel House

A single-storey house where the only structural steel is a ridge beam (the spine) on pillars every 6 m. The walls are 100 mm IBR / EPS cold-room panels standing in folded U-tracks, and the roof panels span from the walls up to the spine. Two versions: A with the door in the long wall, B with a glass slider in the gable end and a covered verandah.

How it goes up

  1. Footing: a slab or strip footing under all the walls, with a pad under each pillar. The base U-track is bolted down round the edge.
  2. Steel: the pillars stand on their base plates and the 200 × 100 ridge beam sits on their cap plates. In version B the Gable A pillar stands on a welded door frame instead, and the spine runs on over the verandah to an open steel A-truss.
  3. Walls: the 1.2 m wall panels stand in the base track and a top U-track caps them. The panels carry the roof edge and hold the house square, so keep enough solid panel in every wall.
  4. Roof: the roof panels span 3.46 m from the wall track up to the spine, screwed at both ends, with a 300 mm eave overhang.
  5. Windows, door or glass slider into the panel openings; ridge capping, barges and trims.

How it works

Spine, pillars and panel walls

The ridge beam is held up by pillars, so the roof does not push the walls outwards and there are no rafters, ties or purlins. The walls are load-bearing panels held top and bottom by light folded U-tracks, as in a cold room. Structural steel for version A is about 0.33 t.

Will the door frame flex?
Downwards, not much. The short pillar brings about 6 kN (≈ 600 kg) from the spine onto the middle of the 3.6 m head. A slider jams if the head sags more than about 1/500 of the span, roughly 7 mm. A 200 × 100 × 4 head, the same section as the spine, should sag about 2 mm.
Sideways is the real problem. The glass removes 3 of the 5 panels that hold the house square at that end. The door frame has to take over: welded or haunched corners, and each leg bolted to its own pad with four bolts so it cannot rock.
Wind on the glass. 7.6 m² of glass catches a lot of wind. The slider needs a wind rating, and the verandah roof shelters it.
First estimates for the engineer to check, not a design.

Open questions

  • Panel figures from the factory: load a 2.4 m wall panel can carry, roof panel span (3.46 m here), and racking strength of a gable wall.
  • Engineer: spine size (200 × 100 × 4 RHS assumed), pillar size, cap and base plates, roof tie-down for wind uplift.
  • Version B: welded door frame design (corners and leg bases) and the verandah A-truss.
  • Gable panel layout: start each gable with a half panel so a joint lands on the centre pillar.
  • Price and availability of folded U-track and of 50 mm panel for internal partitions.
